# Barzinho — Operating Diligence: the questions for Karen

> Supporting doc for `proposal-for-karen.md`. The proposal's §5 is the short, Karen-facing
> subset; **this is the full bank** behind it — the operational questions across beverage,
> food, staff and events whose answers actually set what 75% of Net Profit is worth and
> whether Jamie can run the place. Built from four research passes (June 2026) reconciled
> against `confirmed-facts.md` (L4) and `market-analysis.md` (L3).
>
> **Reading the figures.** Anything tagged **[benchmark]** is an industry/market guidepost —
> context for the question, *not* a Barzinho number. Anything tagged **[PT]** is a Portuguese
> legal/tax point to confirm with the *advogado* / *TOC*. Per the workspace rule, **no Barzinho
> financial is invented here** — the blanks are exactly what these questions ask Karen to fill.
> **★** marks the deal-defining questions: the answers that move the Net Profit pool, the money
> mechanics, or Jamie's ability to operate.

---

## 0. Why this matters — the through-line

On a share-of-Net-Profit deal, **every leak is Jamie's leak**. A few points of pour-cost
slippage, an over-staffed winter, a delivery channel that loses money per order, a live-music
budget that doesn't wash its face — none of it shows in headline revenue, all of it comes
straight out of the 75%. So the questions below aren't curiosity; each one is a line that
either feeds the shared pool, prices a risk Jamie now carries himself (there is no floor), or
is a liability Jamie could walk into.

Three cross-cutting things to extract everywhere:
- **What does it cost** (so it's correctly in/out of the Net Profit definition)?
- **Whose name is it in, and does it transfer to Jamie** for the trial (contracts, licences,
  relationships, accounts)?
- **What liability comes attached** (arrears, disputes, clawbacks, compliance gaps)?

## 1. Beverage program

*The margin engine. Bar GP is where the profit is made — blended pour cost should sit around
**18–24%** **[benchmark]**, and acceptable stock variance is **1–2%**; badly run bars leak
**5–25%** **[benchmark]**. The biggest hidden trap is a brewery tie-in on loaned equipment.*

**Contracts & exclusivity**
1. ★ Is there a **draft/keg supply or exclusivity contract** (Sagres, Super Bock, Nortada,
   other)? Can I see the **signed agreement** — end date, auto-renewal, and any **minimum
   annual volume commitment**? *This can lock the menu, the supplier and the price for years.*
2. ★ Who **owns the draft equipment** (font, cooler, taps), the fridges, glassware and branded
   signage/umbrellas — Barzinho, or the brewery **on loan**? If loaned, what's the **clawback**
   if the brand is dropped? *[benchmark: tie-in kit is usually loaned, not owned.]*
3. Are there other supplier tie-ins (spirits, wine, soft drinks, coffee) with exclusivity or
   rebate-for-volume terms I'd be bound to?

**Suppliers, terms & money**
4. Who actually delivers each category, and what are the **credit terms** (cash-on-delivery vs
   30 days), minimum orders and delivery frequency? *Credit often doesn't transfer to a new
   operator — a month-1 working-capital hit.*
5. ★ What **rebates / volume discounts / retro-bonuses** does Barzinho earn today, and are they
   paid **to the business or to you personally**? *If personal, they vanish from the pool I share.*
6. Are there outstanding **keg/glassware deposits or supplier balances** I'd inherit, and is
   anything on sale-or-return?
7. [PT] Is Barzinho registered for the **SDR deposit-return scheme** (mandatory from Aug 2026),
   and who handles that compliance?

**Margins, pricing & stock**
8. ★ What is the current **pour cost / GP by category** (draft, bottle, spirits, cocktails,
   wine, softs)? Can I see **cost prices and the price list together**?
9. ★ What's the current **stock variance / shrinkage %**, and how is it measured today? *This
   number is money straight off my 75%.*
10. Will we do a **signed, itemised opening stocktake at cost on day one**, and who values
    out-of-date or unsaleable stock? *Baseline every future month's GP is measured against.*
11. What's the **line-cleaning schedule and who does it**, when were lines last cleaned, and
    roughly how many **kegs/week** in and out of season?
12. [PT] Are menu prices **net or gross of 23% IVA**, and how are mixed-rate items invoiced?
    *Drinks (incl. softs) are 23%; reading the P&L wrong overstates margin.*

**Licensing (alcohol)**
13. ★ In whose name are the **alcohol licence and the Câmara de Mafra operating licence** held,
    and do they stay valid with me running the bar on *recibos verdes*?
14. Any open issues, fines or inspections from **ASAE / GNR / PSP**, or *horário* (hours)
    restrictions on the licence?

## 2. Food & kitchen

*Food rarely is the profit centre — its job is to extend dwell time and pull the drinks order.
Bar food cost runs **28–35%** (GP **65–70%**) **[benchmark]**; the "value burger" is almost
certainly a popular, thin-margin **plowhorse** that only pays if drinks attach to it. Delivery
and kitchen payroll are where the real margin risk sits.*

**Menu profitability & data**
15. ★ Can you share **item-level sales data** (units per dish, last 6–12 months)? *Mix, not the
    headline food-cost %, decides whether the kitchen makes money.*
16. ★ What's the current **food cost % / GP**, and do **costed recipes (plate costs)** exist, or
    do I rebuild them?
17. ★ What's the **average spend per cover** and the **drink-attach rate to food orders** — do
    food customers also drink, or come just for the cheap burger? *The whole case for keeping a
    low-margin food menu.*
18. Which dishes are your **best-margin** items and which are loss-leaders kept for footfall?
    What's the **food-vs-drinks revenue split**?

**Kitchen staff** *(see also §3 — the single biggest food-side risk)*
19. ★ How many **kitchen staff**, in what roles/hours, at what **total cost**, and on what
    contracts? Will the **current cook(s) stay** after handover?
20. Are any **recipes/techniques dependent on one person** who could leave? Any kitchen-staff
    liabilities (owed wages, holiday, severance) I'd inherit?

**Food cost, suppliers, delivery**
21. Who are the **suppliers** (Makro, local fish/produce/bakery), and do **prices and credit
    terms transfer**, or do I start on cash? *[benchmark: food credit is personal, rarely
    transfers.]*
22. What's the typical **monthly food spend**, how **volatile** are fresh inputs (gambas,
    produce, dairy), and how is **wastage** tracked? *[benchmark: 4–10% of purchases wasted.]*
23. ★ Which **delivery platforms** are we on, and what are the **exact commission rates**
    (Glovo / Uber Eats / Bolt Food)? *[PT/benchmark: 25–35%, effective up to ~40% with add-ons
    — many delivery orders are break-even or loss-making.]*
24. ★ What **share of food revenue** is delivery/takeaway vs dine-in, and is delivery actually
    **profitable per order** after commission and packaging? Are the contracts in **your name
    or the business's**, and do they **transfer**?

**Equipment & compliance** *[PT]*
25. What kitchen **equipment is owned vs leased**, what's its age/condition (fryers, grill,
    fridges, freezers, extraction), and any imminent replacements or service contracts?
26. ★ Is there a **current, documented HACCP plan** and who maintains it? Is the **municipal
    restauração licence** valid and **in whose name**?
27. What's the **ASAE inspection history** — any past infractions, fines or open issues I'd be
    taking on? Are allergen info and temperature/HACCP logs being kept?

## 3. Staff & labour

> **Deal shape (user-confirmed, Jun 2026):** the **2 full-timers are Karen and her partner** —
> they **keep their salaries and stay hands-off**, so that salary is **owner-drawings added back**
> to the Net Profit pool (§2–3 of the proposal), *not* a payroll cost Jamie carries. The
> **part-time staff are Jamie's to hire, fire and schedule at his discretion within an agreed
> monthly budget, paid by the business (Karen's cost)**. So the heavy per-employee diligence
> below is **superseded** — what matters now is (a) the add-back figure, (b) the part-time budget,
> (c) who carries employment liability. The original questions are kept struck-through as
> reference in case the structure shifts.

*Because Jamie controls part-time hours, the seasonality risk is largely his to manage (flex
labour down in winter). The fixed cost that doesn't flex is the owners' salary — but that's added
back, so it doesn't shrink his pool. Target labour cost **25–35% of revenue** **[benchmark]**.*

**Live questions (current deal shape)** *[PT]*
28. ★ Confirm the **two full-timers are Karen + partner**, that they **retain their salaries and
    stay hands-off**, and **what that salary figure is** — it's the **add-back quantum** that
    sets the size of the shared pool.
29. ★ **Agree the monthly part-time staffing budget** Jamie hires and schedules within, paid by
    the business. What does the current part-time wage run cost, as a starting anchor?
30. ★ **BARZA remains the sole legal employer** of all staff, carries payroll / **23.75% TSU** /
    social security / **any severance**, and **indemnifies Jamie** against employment claims —
    even where Jamie initiates a hire or dismissal within budget. Confirm in writing.
31. ★ For existing part-timers Jamie may inherit then release: what **severance / notice cost**
    would land (Portugal is hard-to-fire — 12 days/yr + seniority **[PT]**), and does that come
    out of **Karen's cost / the budget**, not Jamie's share?
32. Has Karen taken Portuguese advice on whether Jamie's full-time, on-site **recibos-verdes**
    role risks reclassification as **employment (*falso recibo verde*)**, and who bears that
    exposure? *A live structural risk on Jamie's own side of the deal — unaffected by the staff
    changes.*
33. Any **side-letters or verbal promises** to existing part-timers (pay, hours) Jamie would be
    expected to honour, and is everyone properly **registered with Social Security**?

**Superseded** — the original full per-employee bank, kept only in case the structure shifts back
to inheriting an employee team:

- For **each** employee: role, contract type, start date/seniority, gross wage, €920-minimum vs
  CCT-category rate.
- Total fully-loaded monthly payroll + annualised incl. 13th/14th month; 12 months of payroll runs.
- Which CCT applies (AHRESP–SITESE?) and each employee's category / real wage floor.
- Meal allowance (cash €6.15 / card €10.46/day) and any above-statutory perks.
- Accrued-but-untaken holiday per employee; seniority liabilities on long-tenured staff.
- Any disputes, ACT complaints, grievances, long-term sick-leave, pending claims.
- Whether staff intend to stay; fixed-terms about to expire/convert; seasonal contracting in place.

## 4. Events & live music

> **Deal shape (user-confirmed, Jun 2026):** Jamie wants the **contact book of all past events**
> (acts, DJs, dance instructor, promoters) handed over and **full freedom to plan the calendar**.
> So the framing is **acquire-the-asset + freedom-to-run**, not just "does the network transfer."
> The events budget sits inside Jamie's operating budget. The compliance questions (royalties,
> noise) still stand — they're liabilities the operator carries regardless.

*Events make money at the **bar, not the door**: a €250–400 act needs **~60–90 incremental
drinks** just to break even **[benchmark]**, so the events budget is a **marketing cost line**.
The dominant risk is the **23:00 noise window** for a late-music venue.*

**The asset Jamie wants + the budget**
42. ★ Hand over the **contact book for all past events** — acts, DJs, the dance instructor,
    promoters — and confirm **Jamie has full freedom to plan the calendar** (which events run,
    and how). A handover **introduction** to the regulars and the dance instructor.
43. ★ The **events budget** sits inside Jamie's operating budget. As an anchor: what do the acts,
    DJs and the Friday dance night currently cost (flat fee / door split / drinks)?
44. Any **written residencies/agreements** with acts or promoters, or commitments already booked
    beyond the trial that Jamie would inherit?

**Music royalties** *[PT]*
45. ★ Are the **SPA and PassMúsica licences** current and paid, in whose name, and can I see the
    latest invoices? *Both are mandatory even with free entry; liability sits with the operator.*
46. Is Barzinho classified as having a **"dance floor"** for tariff purposes (which jumps the
    SPA tariff sharply — indicatively ~€1,200/yr without vs ~€7,700/yr with **[PT-tariff]**),
    and what **annual royalty** are you actually paying? Any **arrears or disputes**?

**Noise licence & complaints** *[PT]*
47. ★ What does Barzinho's **utilisation/operating licence permit** for amplified/live music,
    and **until what hour** can we legally run music? *The 23:00–07:00 window is the venue's
    biggest regulatory exposure.*
48. Do you hold / routinely apply for a **Licença Especial de Ruído** for late or louder events,
    and how does Câmara de Mafra treat these?
49. ★ What's the **noise-complaint history** — neighbour complaints, GNR/ASAE call-outs, fines
    or conditions, especially for music past 23:00?

**Esplanada / outdoor** *[PT]*
50. Is the **esplanada licence current** (zone, hours, fees, seasonality) in Mafra, and **can any
    music or events run outdoors** — or does outdoor amplified sound breach the esplanada
    conditions or trigger noise complaints?

## 5. Money mechanics, entity & insurance *(completeness pass, 2026-07)*

> Added after reviewing the proposal against the agreement template (Clauses 5, 7, 9, 19, 22).
> These are the payment-plumbing and protection questions the operational sections above don't
> cover — most are now asked directly in proposal §3–§5.

51. ★ **Which entity does Jamie invoice** each month — Karen personally, or BARZA? Which entity
    actually runs the operation's books (the P&L shows IRC)? **[PT — TOC]** *Changes IVA and
    bookkeeping treatment; must be settled before the first invoice.*
52. ★ **Payment mechanics:** who prepares the monthly Net Profit figure, off what agreed
    one-page template, and by when is Jamie's share paid (proposed: the 15th of the following
    month)? The other party checks; the **TOC's figure settles disagreements**.
53. ★ **Negative months:** confirmed stance — a monthly loss **carries forward** against future
    profit before anything is shared. Without this, seasonality games the monthly split.
54. **Day-to-day money handling:** till counts, banking, and how Jamie pays suppliers within
    budget (business card with a limit vs payments released on his instruction). Who holds bank
    access? *In a cash-heavy bar this is the biggest single leak-point.*
55. **Insurance:** is cover current and paid — public liability, contents, workplace-accident
    cover (*seguro de acidentes de trabalho*) — in whose name, at what cost? **[PT]** *A
    running-cost line for the Schedule-1 list, and an uninsured incident is a pool-destroying
    liability.*
56. ★ **Working capital to reopen:** the bar has been closed the past month — what does
    reopening cost (restock, cleaning, repairs), is anything owed from before, and do
    **pre-trial reopening costs sit with Karen**, not the shared pool?
57. **Tips:** how are they handled today (individual, pooled, kitchen share)? Keep the team's
    current practice unless agreed otherwise — morale, not margin.
58. ★ **The real accounting** behind the modeled P&Ls: the *balancetes* or year-end accounts
    from the TOC. *The supplied PDFs are illustrative; the baseline needs source figures.*
59. **Seasonality-fair trial review:** agree up front that trial months are read against the
    **same months in the baseline**, not the annual average — fair in both directions.
