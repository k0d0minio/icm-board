## The offer in one sentence

> A senior engineer who looks at how your business actually runs, sorts which parts belong
> to software, to an assistant, to a person or to nothing, builds what makes sense at a
> fixed price, and hands you a repository that carries its own way of working — so your
> team, or any developer, can carry on without him.

*— draft, Jamie to confirm (Q22).* Until he does, the site copy derives from this sentence
and says so in its PR; the sentence changes here first, then everywhere that cites it.

## The three verbs

**Look, then sort.** Every engagement starts with a look at how the business runs today —
a call, then one page: what I saw, where each part of the work belongs, three things I would
do first, how I would start. The sort has four outcomes and each is said plainly: *this could
run itself* (software), *keep a person on it* (judgment, relationships, taste), *worth an
assistant* (a person with a tool that does the repetitive half), *don't build this* (it is
not a problem, or not yet). The look is free after the first call; for a larger or messier
system the sort is a paid diagnostic, credited against the build
([services.md](services.md) § The diagnostic).

**Build what makes sense.** Fixed scope, fixed price, three options to choose between, and
the person on the call is the person who builds it. The stack is the house default
([stack.md](stack.md)) unless the deal says otherwise; the scope is written as outcomes the
client can read, with what is not included said kindly and early. No time-and-materials,
no open-ended retainer without a written monthly scope ([pricing.md](pricing.md)).

**Hand it over, governed.** The client owns the code, the accounts and the repository
(or Jamie hosts on a stated monthly line — [terms.md](terms.md)). The repository carries
its own interpretable pipeline: stages with human gates, a release record for every merge,
an environment declared once and checked on every change, and a way back when a release
goes wrong. Their team, or any developer, can run it — the method is in the folder tree,
not in Jamie's head.

## The method

The way the work is organised — folders as the architecture, markdown contracts as the
interface, a human gate at every boundary — is built on the **Model Workspace Protocol**
(Van Clief & McDermott, 2026, arXiv 2603.16021): an open method, cited as such. No
affiliation with eduba or with the paper's authors is claimed or implied anywhere; the
business applies the method, it did not write it. **Before any public copy ships that names
it, the paper's current title and the method's name are re-checked against arXiv** — the
paper renamed itself once already (its v1 name was Interpretable Context Methodology), and
copy that names a stale title reads as careless. The estate's own vocabulary stays **ICM** —
in contracts, scripts, folder names and every internal file; the public name is for public
surfaces only.

## Three registers

The same offer, said three ways. The vocabulary changes; the floor, the anchor and the
method do not ([pricing.md](pricing.md) § What never appears publicly).

| | Local operating business | SME with a process worth automating | Enterprise |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who** | a café, a venue, a practice, a shop — customers today, one owner who decides | a company with a real process (quotes, onboarding, reporting, scheduling) done by hand or across five tools | a team inside a larger organisation, with procurement, security review and a committee |
| **Vocabulary** | your bookings, your menu, your customers, the page people find you on | your process, your team's week, what the tool does on its own, what still needs a person | the workflow, the integration, the audit trail, governance, handover |
| **The proof they need** | that it works and looks right; someone they know vouched | that the process is understood before it is built; a written look at *their* business | a method they can read and audit; a reference; the repository carrying its own process |
| **The entry offer** | a free look | a free look, then a diagnostic | a diagnostic or a workshop |
| **Price posture** | a number on the page (the landing page from the floor) | a range on the call, never on the page | never on the page — a diagnostic first, then a quote |
| **Words never used** | platform, architecture, pipeline, AI (unless they say it first) | disruption, transformation, synergy, "leverage AI" | cheap, quick, hack, prototype |
| **One real sentence Jamie would say to them** | — not yet established (Q23) | — not yet established (Q23) | — not yet established (Q23) |
| **One word he would never use with them** | — not yet established (Q23) | — not yet established (Q23) | — not yet established (Q23) |

The referral site speaks to the first column through the professional who refers; the
portfolio speaks to the first two; the third is reached by introduction only.

## What never appears publicly

Ever, on any page, form, deck or message a lead can read:

- the internal hourly anchor ([pricing.md](pricing.md) § The anchor);
- the floor as a number *except* as the landing page's "from" price — the one place it is
  a price and not a rule;
- the fixed-price bands, the retainer range, the diagnostic bands and their credit windows —
  each is quoted on a call or in a proposal, never listed;
- a client's name as a reference before their launch, and never without asking;
- anything that names a percentage of anything (equity, commission, revenue share).
