# Karen's counter — analysis, decisions & draft reply

> Working note, not legal advice. Karen has accepted the skeleton but re-architected the
> economics and the legal structure. This maps every negotiation item, gives a recommended
> stance + the alternatives on each, and ends with a draft WhatsApp reply built on the
> recommendations. Swap-points in the draft are marked `[[like this]]` so you can flip a
> decision without rewriting.

---

## The one thing to see first

Karen improved her position on **three axes at once**, and bundled them so they look like one
reasonable ask:

1. **Guaranteed €2,500/month** — she's now covered on rent *regardless of trade*. Under your
   version she carried the asset risk; now your company does.
2. **Bigger profit slice** — 35% vs the 25% you offered.
3. **Fixed-cost risk shifted entirely to you** — under the sub-lease your company owes rent,
   utilities, staff and suppliers *every month, whether or not the bar makes money*.

Your "no floor" was fair **because you had no fixed costs** — worst case you earned zero. Under
her structure, worst case you **pay €2,500 + costs out of your own pocket** in a dead winter
month. That is a materially worse risk position, and it's the hinge of the whole reply.

The core counter-logic: **a landlord who is guaranteed rent AND takes 35% of the upside is
double-dipping.** She can have the *security* of fixed rent with a smaller profit slice, or a
*bigger* profit slice with shared downside — not both at full strength. That single argument
threads items 2, 3 and 7 below.

## The negotiation items

### 1. Structure → business sub-lease (*cessão de exploração*)
**Recommend: accept in principle, conditional on the economics and a TOC sign-off.**
It's not a bad structure for you: cleaner operating control, and it actually *reduces* your own
disguised-employment (*falso recibo verde*) exposure — you're a business operating a business,
not an individual invoicing for labour. But don't concede the structure and the economics in the
same breath. "Happy with the sub-lease frame — let's get the numbers inside it right."
*Alternatives:* push back to the clean profit-share; or make it fully conditional on the TOC.

ANSWER: Sub-leasing was never part of the verbal agreement, the number one condition for this deal to work was for me to take on no financial risk/liability. We must outright refuse this and stick to the recibos verdes setup. I'm also pretty confident that it is illegal to sublet without the original leaser's agreement and consent.

### 2. €2,500/month fixed rent — confirm the quantum
**Recommend: confirm the actual head-lease rent Karen pays Afonso, and peg €2,500 to it.**
Your proposal had rent at ~€2,200–2,500. If she pays Afonso less than €2,500 and charges your
company €2,500, she pockets the spread as *extra* guaranteed income on top of her 35%. Ask to
see the head-lease figure; the sub-lease rent should match it, not sit above it.
⚠️ unconfirmed: actual rent to Afonso.

ANSWER: there is no world in which I take on the responsibility of the risk. So there is no situation in which I take on a fixed cost of 2500 at my own personal expense.

### 3. The split — 65/35
**Recommend: counter at 70/30** (hold 75/25 as the opening if you want room to trade).
The argument writes itself: the guaranteed €2,500 already compensates her for stepping back, so
the split shouldn't *also* swing a full ten points her way. She's taking security **and** more
upside. 70/30 concedes something to the "2 years of build / turnkey" point without paying for it
twice.
*Alternatives:* accept 65/35 and bank the concession on loss-months or the sale clause instead;
or offer a **sliding split** (65/35 up to a profit threshold, tilting to you above it) so you're
rewarded for the growth you drive.

ANSWER: the best I can do is 70/30 in my favour, and again there is no situation in which I pay 2500 out of pocket.

### 4. "Neither of us takes salaries from net profit" — the add-back is gone
**Recommend: accept it — but use it to justify holding the split up.**
Under the sub-lease the owners aren't drawing salary from the business, so there's nothing to add
back; the clean version is consistent and fine. But that add-back was the mechanical support for
your 75/25, so its removal *strengthens* the case in item 3 to not also give ground on the %.
**Keep the one add-back that still matters:** related-party costs above market rate (anything
routed via Afonso beyond the €2,500) must still be added back, exactly as in your §3.

ANSWER: neither of them takes salaries from net profit is agreed, but there is no world in which I pay 2500 out of pocket for sub-leasing

### 5. Operating entity + company creation (Rauva)
**Recommend: accept in principle; make the setup and accounting a pre-split business cost.**
It's the natural consequence of the sub-lease and genuinely de-risks you on employment. But a
company + business bank account + monthly *TOC*/bookkeeping is a **real new cost** that didn't
exist in your version. Agree that company setup and running accounting are **operating costs of
the business, taken off the top before the split** — not a personal cost you swallow.

ANSWER: the trial period was supposed to have a setup that allows me to test out running Barzinho without launching a whole new business. I currently run a freelancer activity here in Portugal, I will firmly stick to the recibos verdes setup.

### 6. Staff on *recibos verdes*, carried by your company
**Recommend: accept the flexibility, flag the risk, ring-fence the pre-trial staff.**
The *falso recibo verde* exposure on staff now sits with **you**, not BARZA — the *advogado* must
bless how staff are engaged. And any staff who **predate the trial** (accrued holiday, past
liabilities) stay Karen's problem — tie that into the §5 clean-base indemnity she's already
accepted.

ANSWER: due to the requirement of sticking to the recibos verdes profit sharing structure, I will not have an entity that can handle staff. So the staffing and liabilities that follow needs to stay with Barza for the duration of this trial.

### 7. Loss months — the crux
**Recommend: rent must defer or pause in any loss month.** This is the non-negotiable.
If the structure makes your company carry all fixed costs, then in a month the bar runs a loss
you should **not** also be funding Karen's €2,500 out of your own pocket. Ask that the sub-lease
rent **defers (repaid from later profit) or pauses** in any loss-making month — the same
carry-forward logic she already accepts for profit, applied to the rent. Given the bar's been
**closed a month**, also ask to **phase the rent in** over the trial while trade recovers.
*Alternatives:* accept the risk and lean on your control of part-time staff cost to flex down; or
accept it paired with a **short break clause** (2 weeks' notice) so you can exit before the
exposure compounds.

ANSWER: There is no real negotiation here as I have already stated that I cannot and will not accept a sub-lease structure that puts me on the line for 2500 every month. 

### 8. Selling later — ROFR + finder's fee vs your uplift-share
**Recommend: accept the ROFR and finder's fee, but keep a slim share of the value you build.**
The right of first refusal and the finder's bonus are genuine wins — take them. But neither pays
you for value you build that a **third party** buys; that's what your uplift clause was for. Keep
a modest uplift-share on the table, *or* trade it away only in exchange for pinning the buy-in
path down concretely (not "to be discussed"). Don't let the clause vanish for nothing.

ANSWER: Accept the ROFR and the finder's fee. Set the finder's fee at a reasonable percentage of the purchase price.
