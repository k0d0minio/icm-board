# Discovery interview — the script

*Layer-3 reference for **the paid diagnostic** — the full interview arc, 30–45 minutes per
sitting over its two weeks ([services.md](../../../_system/knowledge/services.md)). The
first call's shorter crib is [`call-crib.md`](call-crib.md), distilled from § Before, The
craft and the first four beats of the arc below. A crib sheet for a conversation, not a
form to read aloud. Distilled 2026-08-28 from the estate's
real discovery work: berceo's assessment (the pare-down and the gates) and messy-play's
interview-as-prompt (the craft rules).*

## Before — five minutes of prep

Re-read `01-intake.md` **and anything they sent** — a spec, a deck, an old site, a
prototype. Ground every question in what's actually there; never re-ask what a document
already answers. Where a written spec exists, list its internal contradictions first —
each unresolved pair changes the architecture or the estimate, and resolving the top
ones *is* the meeting (S8).

Write the prep list — the short list: **the questions to ask
if we only get one meeting**, chosen by ID from
[`discovery-questions.md`](discovery-questions.md), `[BLOCKER]`s first.

## The craft — how to ask (the messy-play rules)

- **Rounds of at most 3–5 questions**, fewer if they're big. Never the whole bank at
  once. If an answer opens a more important thread, follow it and come back.
- **Choices get options with a recommendation first**, each with a one-line trade-off;
  open-ended things (their story, their frustrations) get plain questions.
- **Interrogate vague answers.** "Modern and clean", "easy to use", "like my
  competitor's" are starting points: what specifically, show me an example, what would
  you hate, what does good look like six months in?
- **Accept "I don't know."** Say what we'd recommend and why, get a yes/no on the
  recommendation, and record it as an *assumption* — flagged, challengeable — never as
  a decision.
- **Replay the brief back** before leaving: purpose, audience, the conversion, must-
  haves, constraints, the assumption list. Let them correct it. Do not skip this.
- **Track every unresolved thing** with who answers it and what it blocks.

## The arc — 30–45 minutes

1. **Their story first.** "Tell me about the business, then about the thing you want
   built." Do not interrupt with solutions. The job here is their words — the register,
   the proposal and half the tickets will quote them.
2. **The job to be done.** What must this thing do for it to have been worth paying
   for? What happens today instead — the manual process, the workaround, the lost
   customer?
3. **Done looks like.** The single sentence they'd accept as "shipped". Push past
   features to outcomes — then S7: if only three things could ship, which three?
4. **The short list.** Work through the prep doc's `[BLOCKER]`s. These are the
   questions a responsible quote cannot be written without — do not leave without
   them. Two that hide: C6 (third-party contracts and their real lead times — the
   build is often not the critical path) and C7 (actual prices for anything sold —
   billing can't be quoted from "TBD").
5. **Constraints, lightly.** Deadline and why · budget shape (a range signal, not a
   negotiation) · content and materials — who writes, who photographs · anything
   `[LAWYER]` (their sector's rules, data they hold — where licensing might apply,
   say plainly that a written opinion from *their* counsel gates launch).
6. **How it should feel** (D1–D5) — three words for the feel, three for the must-not,
   references with the *specifically what*.
7. **What happens next, said plainly.** "I'll send a written proposal by <day>. It
   will have a number in it." No number in the room unless it is the floor
   conversation. For anything spec-shaped and contradictory, the honest offer is a
   paid discovery sprint whose output is the resolved scope plus a fixed quote (R4).

## After — same day

Raw notes into the engagement's `raw/` while they're warm; the diagnostic's report
([`diagnostic-report-template.md`](diagnostic-report-template.md)) is written from them. Anything promised in the room ("I'll send you X") goes in the
notes' top section so it cannot evaporate. The internal K questions get answered
honestly before the quote is written — that answer is allowed to be "we walk".
