# Proposal — Jamie to run Barzinho on a 3-month trial

**From:** Jamie  **To:** Karen  **Re:** Running Barzinho full-time for a share of the profit

> **This is a working draft, not a final agreement and not legal advice.** It sets out what
> I'm proposing and the things we'd need to agree. Several terms are left open on purpose —
> they firm up once you've answered the questions at the end. Before anything is signed, a
> Portuguese *advogado* (and an accountant / *TOC*) should review it.

## 0. Ground rules — what has to hold for this to work (please read first)

Your reply reshaped the structure, so before the detail, here's what's fixed for me and what
I've moved on. These are the conditions the trial has to meet — everything in §1–§8 sits on top
of them.

**The non-negotiables**

1. **I take on no financial risk or liability.** This was the premise from our first
   conversation. I run the bar and take a share of what it makes; I don't take on fixed costs,
   debt, or the lease. In a quiet month I earn little or nothing — but I never end a month
   *owing* money.
2. **Light setup — *recibos verdes*, no new company.** I already operate as a freelancer on
   *recibos verdes* here in Portugal, which is the proportionate way to test the place for three
   months. I invoice monthly for my profit-share. No company creation.
3. **Not a business sub-lease.** The *cessão de exploração* route doesn't work — it puts the rent
   and every running cost on my side each month regardless of trade, which is the exact risk this
   structure is meant to avoid. (A sub-lease would likely need Afonso's written consent in any
   case.) We keep the original shape: **you hold the lease; I manage and take a profit-share.**
4. **Rent is a cost of the business,** paid from takings before Net Profit is worked out — not a
   fixed sum I pay you. You keep the lease and the relationship with Afonso.
5. **Staff stay with BARZA.** As I'm not setting up a company, I've no entity to employ anyone.
   Payroll, social security and any staff liabilities **stay with BARZA for the trial**; I direct
   and schedule the team day-to-day within an agreed budget (§4).

**What I've moved on / agreed**

- **Split: 70/30 in my favour** — up from the 75/25 I first proposed, in recognition of the
  turnkey business you've built (§2).
- **Neither of us draws a salary out of the shared profit** (§3) — agreed.
- **Selling later:** your first-right-of-refusal and finder's-fee offer, accepted — finder's fee
  at a reasonable % of the sale price; a buy-in stays a later conversation (§7).
- **The clean-base confirmations** you've accepted — licences in force, no fines/arrears, clean
  compliance — locked in (§5).

## 1. The proposal in brief

I'd run Barzinho full-time as general manager — day-to-day operations and the budget — while
you step back from running it. In exchange:

- **Split:** **70% of the monthly Net Profit to me, 30% to you.** Your 30% is a passive
  owner's return on a business you're no longer running day-to-day — and reflects that you're
  handing me a turnkey business, not an empty room; you keep holding the lease.
- **How I'm paid:** purely by my 70% share of the monthly Net Profit, invoiced (*recibos
  verdes*) — **not** as an employee. I carry my own IVA / IRS / Segurança Social.
- **No extra cost to you:** there's no salary, no retainer and **no guaranteed floor** on my
  side. If the bar makes nothing in a quiet month, I'm paid nothing — I carry that risk, not
  you. The whole point is that you take on no new cost; I take the upside (and the downside)
  of the 70% split.
- **Trial length:** **3 months**, with a written review point at the end. This is the "test
  the place" period — if it's working for both of us, we roll it forward or move to a longer
  term.

No buyout or ownership change is part of this — me buying in is a separate conversation for
later, if the trial goes well. The one forward-looking point I do want to settle now is the
reverse: what happens if *you* sell the business down the line — first refusal for me, and a
finder's fee if I bring the buyer (§7).

## 2. Why a 70/30 split is fair

It comes down to who does the work and what each of us brings:

- During the trial **I supply all the operating labour and management** — I'm there full-time,
  running the bar. That earns the operator's majority.
- **Neither of us draws a salary out of the profit we share** (§3), so the pool stays clean —
  it's the bar's actual profit, split between us, with no owner drawings quietly shrinking it.
- Your **30%** is a passive owner's return for holding the lease and the asset — and it reflects
  that I'm stepping into a **turnkey business you built**: the reputation, the menu, the licence,
  the crowd, the contacts. I'm not starting from zero, and the split recognises that.

So the logic is simple: **I run it, so I take the operator's majority; you keep a clean passive
owner's share** for the business you've handed me to run. That's the move from the 75/25 I first
put to you up to **70/30** — meeting your point that the value you've built deserves more than a
quarter.

## 3. How we'd define "Net Profit"

The percentage only means something once we agree what it's a percentage *of*. My proposed
basis, in plain terms:

- **Net Profit = the bar's income minus an agreed list of running costs** (rent, staff,
  utilities, stock, licences, etc. — a fixed list we both sign off), worked out **before tax,
  and before my 70% share is taken out.** I'm paid only by that share — there's no separate fee
  and no guaranteed floor.
- **Kept out of the cost list** (so they don't quietly shrink the shared pool):
  - **Owner salaries / drawings** — neither of us takes a salary out of the profit we share, so
    no owner pay sits in the running costs dragging the pool down.
  - **Related-party costs above the normal market rate** — anything billed in above what an
    arm's-length supplier would charge.
  - **One-off and capital costs** — big equipment buys and non-recurring items handled
    separately, not against the monthly profit.
- **When and how I'm paid:** we work each month's figure out from the till/POS reports and that
  month's invoices, off a one-page template we agree up front — one of us prepares it (your
  accountant or me, whichever you prefer), the other checks it — and my share is paid by the
  **15th of the following month**.
- **Loss-making months:** if a month makes a loss, I'm paid nothing and the **loss carries
  forward** — it comes out of the following months' profit before anything is shared. Neither
  of us takes money out of a pool that isn't really there.
- **If we ever disagree on a month's numbers**, your accountant's (*TOC*'s) figure settles it —
  quicker and cheaper than arguing.
- **Open books:** I'd want straightforward access to the till/POS reports, bank statements and
  invoices on reasonable notice. With no ownership stake on my side, this is just so the
  monthly numbers are verifiable for both of us — nothing adversarial.
- **A worked example before we start:** once we've agreed the cost list (§6), we put one real
  month through this definition on a single page, so we both see exactly what counts as a cost
  and what each of us banks. Numbers settle arguments better than wording.

## 4. Trial mechanics

- **Start / end:** a fixed 3-month window (dates to agree), then a written review.
- **My authority:** full control of day-to-day operations and spending within an agreed
  operating budget, with a per-item ceiling above which I check with you first. **That includes
  the team:** I **hire, fire and schedule the part-time staff at my own discretion, within an
  agreed monthly staffing budget, with their wages carried by the business — BARZA staying the
  legal employer (§6).** You and your partner stay hands-off, and neither side draws a salary out
  of the profit we share (§3). A short list of things stays with you — taking on debt, big
  capital spend, and the lease.
- **How money moves day to day:** we'd settle the practical setup before day one — till counts
  and banking, and how I actually pay suppliers within the budget (a business card with a
  limit, or payments you release on my instruction). Mundane, but if it isn't settled the
  budget control means nothing.
- **If either of us walks away:** a notice period (say **two weeks** during the trial — enough
  to hand over cleanly without trapping either of us), and on exit I'm paid out any
  profit-share I've earned but not yet been paid. Light-touch confidentiality both ways.
- **Reading the results:** three months is one season, not a year. At the review we compare
  the trial months against the **same months in the baseline P&Ls**, not the annual average —
  that keeps the read fair in both directions.

## 5. What needs to be true before I start

I'm paid purely out of the profit and I hold no stake, so I can't take on problems that started
before me. A few things need to be confirmed — on paper, not just word — before the trial clock
starts. None of it is unusual; any operator stepping in would ask for the same. These would sit
in the agreement as your written confirmations as of the start date, and the *advogado* will
have standard wording for them.

- **All licences, permits and registrations in force.** The business holds everything it needs
  to trade legally — food & drink, the late licence, the esplanada, live / amplified music, and
  anything else required — each one **valid, current, and in your (or BARZA's) name**, and I'd
  see them before day one. If any is expired, provisional, or needs a **renewal, assessment,
  inspection or works to stay valid**, that gets resolved **and paid for by you** — before the
  start, or on a written timeline we agree — and its cost sits with you, not against the pool we
  share. If it comes to it, the **start date moves** to when they're confirmed in force.
- **No outstanding fines, penalties, arrears or open actions.** As of the start date there are
  no unpaid fines, penalties, back-taxes, arrears, or open complaints, inspections or
  enforcement actions against the business or the premises, and none pending that you're aware
  of. Anything that later surfaces relating to the period **before I start is yours to settle**
  and doesn't come out of the shared profit.
- **Compliance carries forward clean.** The business is — and at the start date will be —
  operating in line with the licences and rules that apply to it, with no known issue that could
  interrupt trading during the trial. If a **pre-existing** gap surfaces mid-trial and stops or
  limits trading, that's not on me: the affected months **don't count against the review**, and
  any cost to fix it sits with you.
- **Ring-fenced in writing.** A short indemnity ties it together — pre-trial liabilities (fines,
  arrears, licence or compliance failures dated before I start) **stay with you**. The point is
  simple: I operate forward from a clean, legal, fully-licensed base, and don't inherit what
  predates me.

Several of the questions in §6 (licences, complaints/inspections, debt) are how we confirm these
in practice — the answers there become the evidence behind the confirmations above.

## 6. Open questions for you, Karen

for the trial.

**Delivery & takeaway**

## 7. If you sell the business later

Buying in is a separate, later conversation — if the trial goes well and I want to buy, we look
at a **gradual buy-in** then. For now, on a sale to someone else, your offer works for me and
replaces the value-share I'd first floated:

- **First right of refusal.** If a buyer comes forward, I get the first option to **match their
  offer** before you sell to anyone else.
- **Finder's fee.** If I **introduce the buyer who completes**, I get a fee set at a **reasonable
  percentage of the sale price — [% to agree]**.

Both are cleaner than the uplift-share I first raised, and I'm happy with them. This gives me
**no ownership, no stake, and no say over whether or when you sell** — the business, the lease
and the decision stay entirely yours. It's simply first refusal, plus a fee if I'm the one who
brings the buyer. The *advogado* can put both into plain wording when we get there.
