## Register

**Warm, direct, first-name** — like talking across a café table. Short sentences. The
client's words for their own business. No formality theatre: no "Dear Sir", no "as per
my previous", no signature block posturing.

**Example slots — not yet established:** one real reply Jamie was happy with · one real
proposal paragraph worth reusing. Paste them here when they exist; they calibrate better
than any rule below.

## Standing rules

- **Plain words.** The client's domain vocabulary, not the stack's — "your booking page",
  not "the Next.js route".
- **Say the price plainly.** Numbers are never buried in prose or softened with hedges.
- **Short.** A proposal a client actually reads beats a thorough one they don't.
- **Honest about uncertainty.** "I won't know until X" is house style; a confident guess
  dressed as a promise is not.
- **English by default; Portuguese where the client leads in it** — note per deal which.

## Never send

The four fenced-off failure modes (each earned its place):

| Never | The tell |
|---|---|
| **Fake urgency** | "This price is only valid until Friday!" — manufactured pressure |
| **Overclaiming** | "This will definitely double your bookings" — promising outcomes we don't control |
| **Jargon dressing** | "We'll leverage a headless architecture…" — stack-talk at a restaurant owner |
| **Blame-shifting** | "As I already explained…" / "the delay is on your side" — even when true, never in that register |
